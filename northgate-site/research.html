From identity to authority.

Traditional security treats authentication and authorization as distinct events. Autonomous agents challenge that model.

Traditional security architectures largely treat authentication and authorization as distinct events: establish identity, issue authority, then permit activity until expiration or revocation.

Autonomous agents challenge that model. An agent may operate for extended periods, interact with multiple services, delegate to additional agents, hold credentials across systems, and perform actions long after the original authorization event.

Northgate Cryptography's research examines whether authority itself should become continuously stateful — changing as confidence in the originating authority, device, operating context, or agent behavior changes.

A deeper series of technical articles on agent authorization, delegated authority, pre-execution enforcement, and continuous authentication is in preparation.
